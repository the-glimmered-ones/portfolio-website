My Passion

The number one driving force of my creative passion is the developmental disorder known as ADHD. I was diagnosed as a child and it has impacted my life in countless ways. It gives me bursts of motivation at the most random times, and pushes me to create something, anything, and I will not stop working until that itch is scratched.

I began my coding journey young, when my father introduced me to LOGO, a turtle graphics program, around grade 5. I was enamored, and checked out books from the library covering the basics of Python and Ruby (none of which I've retained). It stagnated for a while, just sitting there in the back of my brain, until high school when I decided to take programming classes, focusing on Java. Then on my own time, I began working on the basics of HTML and Javascript.

I have long wanted to make video games that leave a lasting impact on players, inspired by games of my childhood, such as the original Half-Life, or other indie developers such as Team Cherry, who released the widely praised Hollow Knight. With the help of a friend, I started work on a multiplayer web game. It was a very large project for my lack of experience, and took me over a year and thousands of lines of code. I learned many useful things, including how to compile my Typescript for the browser using Bun and how to write server-side code that communicates with players over Websockets.

For the moment though, this remains just a passion. In the future, I plan to graduate with a degree in Computer Science, but I will not be working professionally in the games industry, because that does not provide the creative freedom I require.

Thoughts on Generative AI and LLMs

I will start this section off by saying that generative AI in its current state is not worth using. The biggest Gen-AI products are from companies that are not trustworthy or sustainable. Many of them do everything in their power to push harmful legislation and force data centers into communities that will be destroyed by the overuse of water or electricity.

They cannot be trusted with your personal information, especially Meta who owns the product Muse, has a history of selling loads of personal info collected via Facebook. People in power are usually there because they play dirty, and I will not support that.

Furthermore, generative AI has no place in creative spaces. "AI art" cannot exist, because art is a display of the artist's thoughts, fears, hopes, and dreams. A robot that makes an average of its training data can never compare to a human's original experiences. Many text to image models were also trained off of stolen art, with no permission given, no payment in return, and jumbled together to create a pile of stinking shit that everybody hates because it's ugly.
I hope I've made my point clear
